## S-004 – "What hygienic practices must employees follow under Part 111?"

disease control.

assigning qualified supervisors. Every element in the key is in **21 CFR 111.10**, "What requirements apply for
preventing microbial contamination from sick or infected personnel and for hygienic practices?":

| Key element | 21 CFR 111.10 |
|---|---|
| Disease control | (a), (a)(1)–(2): excluding people who might be a source of microbial contamination; employees notify their supervisor |
| Outer garments | (b)(1) |
| Handwashing | (b)(3) |
| Jewelry | (b)(4) |
| Eating and drinking restrictions | (b)(8): no eating food, chewing gum, drinking beverages or using tobacco products where components, dietary supplements or contact surfaces are exposed |

**Effect:** with the current key, an answer that correctly cites 111.10 loses the citation-exact point, and an answer
citing 111.13 gets credit for a section that says nothing about hygiene.

## M-006 – "What does Part 820 provide with respect to foreign manufacturers that refuse to permit an FDA inspection?"

**Key:** citation 21 CFR 820.1(c); section 801(a) of the FD&C Act. Element: refusal to permit inspection is grounds for
refusal of admission of the device into the United States.

**Current regulation:** the outcome in the key is correct. Since the amended Part 820 (QMSR) took effect on
2 February 2026, 820.1(c) no longer mentions inspections. It reads:

> (c) Foreign manufacturers. A device that is imported or offered for import into the United States is subject to
> refusal of admission to the United States under section 801(a) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act if, among
> other things, it appears to be adulterated as set forth in the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act and its
> implementing regulations.

The link from refusing an inspection to adulteration is now in the statute, **FD&C Act § 501(j) [21 U.S.C. 351(j)]**:

> If it is a drug or device and it has been manufactured, processed, packed, or held in any factory, warehouse, or
> establishment and the owner, operator, or agent of such factory, warehouse, or establishment delays, denies, or
> limits an inspection, or refuses to permit entry or inspection.

**Suggested change:** keep the key, and add FD&C Act § 501(j) as an expected or accepted citation. Suggested element
wording: "Refusing (or delaying, denying or limiting) an FDA inspection makes the device adulterated under FD&C Act
§ 501(j); an imported device that appears adulterated is subject to refusal of admission under section 801(a)
(21 CFR 820.1(c))."

**Effect:** without 501(j), a key that cites only 820.1(c) relies on text the current regulation no longer contains.
Our answer cites 820.1(c) and 501(j), both quoted verbatim.
